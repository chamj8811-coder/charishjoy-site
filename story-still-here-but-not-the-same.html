Still Here, But Not the Same

They were still together. So why did it feel as though they had stopped finding each other?

October 11, 2026A short story about distance and finding a way back

By the time she noticed the silence, it had already learned their routines.

They still woke up in the same home. They still asked whether the other had eaten, remembered what to buy at the grocery store, and knew which side of the bed belonged to whom.

From the outside, nothing looked especially wrong. Inside, their conversations had become lists. Their evenings passed beside each other instead of together.

It hadn’t happened all at once. There was no single afternoon she could point to and say, That was when we changed. There were late shifts. Plans postponed until next weekend. Small disappointments that felt too unimportant to mention, then too old to bring up.

She missed the way he used to tell her the smallest things about his day. He missed how easily they once laughed. Neither knew the other was missing it, too.

One evening, they sat at the kitchen table after dinner. He was looking at his phone. She was looking at the two empty plates.

“Do you ever miss us?” she asked.

He put the phone down. “We’re right here.”

“I know,” she said. “That’s what makes it hard to explain.”

For a moment, he didn’t answer. Then he said something she hadn’t expected.

“I thought if I kept everything going, you’d know I was trying.”

She looked at him. So much of what he did had been for their life together. And yet she had been lonely in it.

“I did know,” she said. “I just didn’t know how to tell you I needed you here with me, too.”

They talked longer than they had in months. Not smoothly. They interrupted each other, misunderstood a few things, and had to begin some sentences again. But neither walked away.

Nothing was repaired that night. The missed moments were still missed. Tomorrow would still come with work, errands, and all the ordinary things that had made them tired.

But the next morning, when she came into the kitchen, he had made coffee and left his phone in another room. He pulled out the chair beside him.

It was a small gesture. It wasn’t the answer to everything.

Still, she sat down.

Maybe they couldn’t become the people they used to be. Too much life had happened for that. But for the first time in a long while, they were curious about who the other had become.

They were still here. Not the same. And this time, they were willing to find out what came next—together.
