The Love That Had Nowhere to Go

An original fictional reflection

November 2, 2026CHARISH JOY

The first thing I told you this morning was that it might rain.

I said it without thinking.

In front of your grave.

I almost laughed.

You used to say I checked the weather too much. "Just look out the window like a normal person."

And here I was, still giving you updates.

The cemetery was crowded—families gathered beneath tents, candles melting in the heat, an older woman offering bottled water to strangers. Everyone was talking at once, like a party the dead were invited to.

I brought sunflowers. The shop didn't have your favorites, and I stood there too long, wondering if you'd mind.

Even now, I wanted to get the flowers right.

People think grief lives in the big moments. The funeral. The empty chair.

Mine lives in the supermarket, when your favorite song comes on over the speakers.

In the message I almost send.

In the funny thing that happens at work, and the one second before I remember you won't be there to hear it.

That's what I miss most. Having you to tell.

Someone who knew why a joke was funny. Who remembered the names of people I'd stopped talking about years ago. Who could understand a whole story from the first three words.

So I told you today.

About the pigeon that got into our office elevator and somehow ended up on the eighth floor. About the bakery on the corner that finally closed.

I told you some days are good now. That I laugh. That I've made new memories I wish you were in.

And then I cried.

Because I remembered how much I loved living with you.

Before I left, I straightened the flowers one last time.

You would have said they looked fine.

I could almost hear you.

I lit a candle, stayed a little longer, and said what I always said when I left the house.

"See you."

I know I won't.

For the people we still love, even when we can no longer tell them.

An original work of fiction. The narrator and events are imagined.
