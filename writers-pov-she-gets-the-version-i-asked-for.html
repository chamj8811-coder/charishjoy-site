She Gets the Version I Asked For

Writer’s POV · How do you stop questioning your worth when someone becomes a better partner for somebody else?

October 26, 2026By CHARISH JOY

I think one of the cruelest parts of heartbreak isn't always losing someone.

Sometimes, it's watching them become the person you spent years hoping they would be.

The patience you kept asking for. The affection you quietly wished for. The little gestures you convinced yourself weren't important.

Suddenly, they seem to be giving those things to someone else.

And before you know it, you start questioning yourself.

Was I too difficult to love?

Did I ask for too much?

Was there something about me that made loving me so hard?

I think that's where heartbreak becomes something deeper than missing a person. It begins to change the way you see yourself.

You replay conversations, remember your mistakes, and wonder whether doing things differently might have changed the ending.

But here's something I've come to understand.

Someone's ability to love another person differently is not proof that you were lacking something.

People grow. Sometimes they learn too late. Sometimes a new relationship brings out a different side of them. And sometimes, what we see from the outside isn't the whole story.

But none of those possibilities should become the measure of your worth.

You can acknowledge your own imperfections without accepting responsibility for every way someone failed to love you.

And maybe that's where moving on begins.

Not by pretending you no longer care. Not by forcing yourself to forgive. And certainly not by competing with the person who came after you.

But by slowly letting go of the need to understand why you weren't given what someone else seems to receive so easily.

Stop searching your past for evidence that you were somehow less deserving.

Stop imagining the version of yourself you think might have made them stay, change, or treat you better.

And stop measuring your worth against a relationship you can only see from the outside.

You are allowed to grieve the years you spent waiting. You are allowed to feel angry about what it cost you to keep hoping things would change.

But you are also allowed to build a life that no longer revolves around their choices.

When I wrote She Gets the Version I Asked For, I wanted to capture that complicated feeling of wishing someone well while quietly grieving the love you never received.

Because sometimes, the hardest part isn't accepting that someone has moved on.

It's accepting that you may never get a satisfying answer to why things were different with you.

And perhaps healing doesn't begin when you finally understand them.

Perhaps it begins when you stop treating the love you didn't receive as evidence that something was wrong with you.

You may never know why they changed, or whether they truly did.

But you don't have to spend the rest of your life questioning your worth because of it.

You deserved tenderness even before they learned how to give it.

— CHARISH JOY
